The Meru Tree Planting Initiative aims to combat deforestation and soil erosion in Meru County by planting 10,000 indigenous trees across 50 hectares of degraded land. This community-led project engages local farmers in sustainable agroforestry practices that will sequester carbon while providing additional income through carbon credits.

The project focuses on native tree species that are well-adapted to the local climate and provide multiple benefits to the ecosystem. These trees will help restore wildlife habitats, improve soil fertility, and enhance water retention in the area.

By investing in this project, you're not only offsetting your carbon footprint but also supporting local livelihoods and biodiversity conservation in Kenya.

Project Gallery

Expected Impact

  • Sequester approximately 5,000 tons of CO2 over 10 years
  • Restore 50 hectares of degraded land
  • Support livelihoods of 25 local farming families
  • Improve soil fertility and reduce erosion
  • Enhance biodiversity and wildlife habitat
Planted 500 Acacia trees in Eastern sector
April 15, 2025

The team successfully planted 500 Acacia trees in the eastern sector of the project area. Soil conditions were favorable and all seedlings were planted with proper spacing and protection.

Installed irrigation system for nursery
April 10, 2025

Completed the installation of a drip irrigation system for the tree nursery. This will ensure consistent water supply to seedlings during the dry season and improve survival rates.

Community training on tree care
April 5, 2025

Conducted a training session for 15 community members on proper tree care techniques, including pruning, mulching, and pest management. All participants received certificates and tool kits.

Site mapping and soil analysis completed
March 28, 2025

Completed comprehensive mapping of the project site and conducted soil analysis across different sectors. Results show varying soil conditions that will inform species selection for different areas.
